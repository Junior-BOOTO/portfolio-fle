# Four-skill assessment framework

## Unit assessment blueprint

Each unit has four **separately recorded** assessments. The action product can supply evidence but does not replace any skill. Use new yet familiar-context texts/audios, not a memorized copy of the lesson. Teacher-created or licensed material only. Provide one practice opportunity, then summative evidence and feedback.

| Skill | Suggested task (A1) | Observable evidence | Access conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO | 30–60 second teacher-recorded message/dialogue; 2–3 plays; 3–5 picture, matching or short-answer items | locates explicit names, actions or choices | clear pace, quality audio, picture supports; record replay/support |
| CE | 35–90 word illustrated notice, chart, card or short message; 3–5 items | locates explicit information and matches meaning | readable layout; vocabulary already taught; text length adjusted for grade |
| PO | 1–2 minute paired exchange or supported individual presentation; 2 prompts and a follow-up | intelligibility, relevant content, response to interlocutor | preparation time, visual prompts; oral support recorded |
| PE | from labels/model sentence (grade 2) to 3–6 connected short sentences (grade 5) | relevant message, comprehensibility, basic familiar language | word bank/model as specified; support level recorded |

These lengths and times are **local design parameters**, not official CEFR examination standards. For grade 2, reduce task size and accept words, labels and sentence frames. For grade 5, maintain A1 access and add an optional richer explanation only when justified by diagnostic evidence.

## Analytic rubric, per skill

Score each criterion **0–3**; use a separate row for each skill, never one combined language mark. 0 = no assessable evidence even with offered access support; 1 = emerging (substantial prompting and limited correct meaning); 2 = developing (main message achieved with some support/errors); 3 = secure for the taught goal (clear, mostly independent performance). Missing evidence is marked **NE** and reassessed; do not equate absence with zero. This is a local formative/summative rubric, not an IB or Council of Europe official rubric.

| Skill | Criterion 1 | Criterion 2 | Criterion 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO | key meaning recognized | explicit details recognized | instruction/question acted on appropriately |
| CE | overall purpose recognized | explicit details located | information used to complete task |
| PO | message and relevance | interaction/response | intelligibility and familiar language |
| PE | communicative content | organization/connection | comprehensibility and familiar forms |

**Scoring note:** score each criterion 0–3 (maximum 9 per skill). Record the task, supports, date, three criterion scores and one next step. Compare a learner with their prior evidence; do not convert a 9-point result into an automatic CEFR certification. Moderate sample performances across teachers before consequential reporting.

## Worked assessment: Grade 5 U1

- **CO:** listen to a new 45-second eco-charter announcement twice; identify two duties and one reason (3 items).
- **CE:** read a short illustrated school charter (about 70 words); locate two rights, two duties and the intended audience (5 items).
- **PO:** with a partner, propose one rule and respond to a question « Pourquoi ? » using a picture prompt; record teacher observations against the PO criteria.
- **PE:** write a three-to-five-sentence eco-charter entry: rule, reason, example and optional invitation. Assess PE independently of the spoken exchange.
- **Reflection:** « Je peux expliquer une règle simple et donner une raison »; choose one next target. An optional extension adds a second reason without changing the core success criterion.
